The split, and why it changes everything

Molecular datasets are dense with near-duplicate scaffolds. Split them at random and close analogues land on both sides, so a model can score well by recognising a skeleton it has already seen. A scaffold split puts every Bemis–Murcko scaffold entirely on one side of the line. The gap between the two is a direct measure of how much a model was memorising.
