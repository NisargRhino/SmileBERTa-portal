Docking and scoring with AutoDock Vina

How SmileBERTa prepares a protein and a ligand, searches for binding poses, and estimates binding affinity, plus how to read the results critically.

What docking answers

Molecular docking predicts how a small molecule sits in a protein's binding site (its pose) and estimates how strongly it binds (a score, in kcal/mol). It is a search problem: the ligand can translate, rotate and flex its rotatable bonds, which gives an enormous number of configurations. A scoring function judges each one, and a search algorithm hunts for the best.

SmileBERTa uses AutoDock Vina 1.1.2 (Trott & Olson, 2010), one of the most widely used and cited docking engines.

Preparing the inputs

  • Receptor cleaning keeps the protein atoms of the chains you select (plus structural metal ions), uses the first model and first alternate location, and discards waters, buffer molecules and co-crystallised ligands so they don't block the pocket.
  • Protonation. X-ray structures rarely include hydrogens. Vina needs polar hydrogens to recognise H-bond donors, so they are added at physiological pH.
  • Ligand 3D generation uses distance-geometry embedding (ETKDG) followed by MMFF94 minimisation, so the starting geometry has realistic bond lengths and ring shapes. Vina keeps those fixed and explores only the torsions.

The search box

Vina only searches inside a rectangular box that you place over the binding site. A good box covers the whole pocket plus a few ångströms of margin. Too small and the correct pose may not fit. Too large and the search gets slower and less reliable. When a structure contains a co-crystallised ligand, SmileBERTa offers to centre the box on it and sizes it to the ligand's extent plus 10 Å (minimum 18 Å per side).

The Vina scoring function

Vina's score is an empirical function fitted to experimental binding data. For every pair of ligand–receptor heavy atoms i, j, it works with the surface distance d = rij − Ri − Rj (the centre-to-centre distance minus both van der Waals radii) and sums five terms:

Einter = Σi,j [ −0.0356·gauss1(d) − 0.00516·gauss2(d) + 0.840·repulsion(d) − 0.0351·hydrophobic(d) − 0.587·hbond(d) ]

The total is then divided by a penalty for ligand flexibility, because rotatable bonds lose freedom when a ligand binds:

affinity (kcal/mol) = Einter / (1 + 0.0585 · Nrot)

How poses are found

Vina uses an iterated local search. Each run starts from a random pose and repeats: randomly perturb the position, orientation or one torsion (a Monte-Carlo step), then refine with a quasi-Newton (BFGS) local optimisation that uses gradients of the score. Improvements are accepted by a Metropolis criterion. Exhaustiveness sets how many such runs execute. Higher values explore more thoroughly but take proportionally longer. The best distinct poses are clustered and returned.

To keep this fast, Vina pre-computes the receptor's contribution on a 3D grid (spacing 0.375 Å) before searching, so scoring a pose becomes a cheap lookup. That is the "computing grid maps" step you see in the log.

Reading the results

ColumnMeaning
Affinity (kcal/mol)Predicted binding free energy ΔG. More negative is better. Drug-like binders typically score −7 to −12.
RMSD l.b. / u.b.Distance of each pose from the best pose. Lower bound matches atoms by symmetry; upper bound matches atom by atom. Poses within about 2 Å are essentially the same binding mode.
H-bondsLigand–protein N/O pairs within 3.5 Å, computed by SmileBERTa from the pose geometry.
Contact residuesResidues with any heavy atom within 4 Å of the ligand, which together define the binding site.

The estimated inhibition constant shown in the tool comes from the thermodynamic relation:

ΔG = RT · ln Ki ⇒ Ki = exp(ΔG / RT), RT ≈ 0.593 kcal/mol at 298 K

So −7 kcal/mol corresponds to roughly 7 µM, and −10 kcal/mol to roughly 50 nM. Treat these numbers as rough orders of magnitude only.

Good practice and limitations

  • Validate with redocking. Dock the co-crystallised ligand back into its own structure. If the top pose is within 2 Å RMSD of the crystal pose, your box and settings are reasonable.
  • Compare, don't trust absolutes. Vina scores rank related compounds better than they predict exact affinities. The typical error is about 2 kcal/mol.
  • The protein is rigid. Induced fit, waters that mediate binding, and entropy are largely ignored.
  • Look at the pose. A good score with no sensible H-bonds, or a ligand half outside the pocket, is a warning sign.
